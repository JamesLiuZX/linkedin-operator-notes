## Draft

Peak-day volume during a marquee event is not evidence of anything.

The number I trust is a ratio: active traders on peak day, divided by active traders on the second Tuesday after the event resolved.

{{RATIO_SENTENCE: state the ratio you actually measured and the window. Example shape: "Last time I measured it the answer was 12 to 1 over a 3 week window." One sentence, no absolute figures.}}

A high ratio means the event rented you a crowd. A low one means some of them found a reason to come back when nothing historic was happening.

Both look identical on peak day. That is the trap. The spike arrives with screenshots, leadership asks how to keep the energy, and the honest answer is that the energy was never yours.

{{COST_SENTENCE: name the thing that got funded off a spike and did not survive the quiet week. One sentence, principle level.}}

What actually carries a market product through the quiet week is a loop that closes without a headline. Watch, decide, position, resolve, learn, return. Break any step and the spike was rented attention with a receipt attached.

The uncomfortable version of the question is not how to make the final bigger. It is what Tuesday looks like after the final, and whether anyone on the team has ever opened that number deliberately rather than finding it in a quarterly review.

Takeaway: measure the second Tuesday, and plan the next event against that number rather than the peak.
