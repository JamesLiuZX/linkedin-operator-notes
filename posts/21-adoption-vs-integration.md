## Draft

88% of contact centers use AI in some form. 25% have fully integrated it into the actual workflow.

Same survey. Same population. Both numbers true at the same time. Most people who quote the first one have never seen the second.

The gap exists because "use AI in some form" is a low bar. A single AI-routing step counts. A chatbot handling the easiest 10% of tickets, everything else going to a human exactly like before, counts. A pilot running next to the real system, generating a dashboard nobody trusts yet, counts. None of that is a workflow that changed. It's a workflow with a feature bolted to the side of it.

The gap doesn't close everywhere at the same speed. 78% of the top 50 banks have a production voice agent live for at least 1 real use case, up from 34% two years ago. A regulated industry with real accountability on every call closed the pilot-to-production gap fast. A side project with nobody's job tied to it has no equivalent pressure to finish.

So the useful question isn't "do you use AI here." It's which of the two numbers a given "yes" actually means, and that depends more on who's accountable for the outcome than on how good the model is.

Takeaway: adoption and integration are 2 different numbers on the same survey. Ask which fraction of the workflow the system runs unassisted, not whether it's switched on somewhere.
