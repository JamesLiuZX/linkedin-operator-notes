# Users forgive a bad beat. They do not forgive feeling hustled.

I have watched people lose money and come back the next morning. I have also watched people win once, feel something was off about the venue, and never open the app again.

Same balance change. Opposite story in their head.

A market product leaks trust in 3 places. Most teams obsess over 1 and quietly bleed from the other 2.

Price has to feel real. If you cannot exit without eating glass, users learn the venue is a toy, and no amount of interface polish reverses that lesson. Fewer liquid markets beat forty decorative ones. Sector open interest was $1.11B on 1 May 2026 against $8.6B of April volume, so about 13% of the headline is real positions. Depth is rarer than volume charts suggest.

Resolution is UX, even when legal owns the document. People accept losing. They do not accept not understanding how the outcome was decided. A $60M dispute over whether Strategy sold Bitcoin in May came down to the word "significant" sitting in a sentence with no test in it.

The app has to stop surprising people. Fees that appear after confirm. PnL that does not match the arithmetic in someone's head. Orders hanging during the only five minutes anyone cared about. In a social app surprise is annoying. In a market it feels extractive.

My own version of this: I shipped a market whose settle explainer I could not write without hedging, and told myself the rules document covered it. The rules document is not a product.

When a roadmap item shows up I ask one thing. Does this make prices feel more real, settles feel cleaner, or the app feel less surprising?

Takeaway: build for the person who lost money fairly and came back anyway. Everyone else is traffic.
