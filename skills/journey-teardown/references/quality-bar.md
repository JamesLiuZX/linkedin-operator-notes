# The quality bar

A teardown from this skill is not handed over until these hold. Six are checked by `scripts/check.py`. One is yours.

| # | Check | Who checks | What fails it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every score has a reason | Script | A missing dimension, a score that is not a whole number from 1 to 5, a reason under four words, or a lane with no verdict |
| 2 | Every pattern lists where it was seen | Script | A pattern naming fewer than two apps or no competitor, an anti-pattern naming none, a stage with no finding, or an ideal flow outside two to five steps |
| 3 | Every gap names a benchmark | Script | A gap missing its priority, what happens today or the fix, or a benchmark app with no lane on the board. A gap with no competitor showing the fix must say so in a note |
| 4 | No screen has more than three notes | Script | A fourth note, a note with an unknown label, a note over 30 words, or a screen with more than one highlighted block |
| 5 | Your product is the last lane in every stage | Script | Any stage where the user's product is not last, lanes out of the app order, or a steal list on your own lane |
| 6 | Coverage is stated | Script | A lane with fewer than two screens and no coverage line, or an app absent from a stage and not listed as missing |
| 7 | Your product was scored by the same rubric | You | Read your own lanes last and ask whether you were kinder to them |

## Why these seven

1. **Reasons with scores.** A bare number invites argument about the number. A reason invites argument about the screen, which is the useful one.
2. **Sightings with patterns.** "Best practice" with no named example is an opinion. Two apps doing it is an observation.
3. **Benchmarks with gaps.** A gap is only actionable if someone can go and look at the fix working.
4. **Three notes.** More than three and nobody reads any of them. The limit forces a choice about what matters on the screen.
5. **Your product last.** The comparison is easiest when the contrast is always in the same place: straight under the best example.
6. **Coverage.** A board looks complete even when it is not. Thin lanes have to say so on their face.
7. **The same rubric for yourself.** The script flags it when your own product outscores every competitor, but it cannot tell you whether that is true.

## What the checker does not see

- Whether a note is right about the screen. It cannot look at pictures.
- Whether a score is fair.
- Whether the screens are current.

Those are the review. Budget half an hour for it.
