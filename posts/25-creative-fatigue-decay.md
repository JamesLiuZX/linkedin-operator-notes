## Draft

The same ad, shown to the same audience enough times, stops working. Not because the product changed. Because the audience has seen it too many times, and CTR decays as a direct function of repetition, a well-documented pattern called creative fatigue.

Smartly.io analyzed 6.7 million ad placements across Instagram and TikTok and found AI-driven creative rotation cut that fatigue-decay rate by 38.4%. A static, non-rotated campaign held above its baseline CTR for an average of 7.1 days. The AI-rotated version held for 19.3 days, almost 3x as long, before the same decay set in.

This is a different claim than the CTR-versus-conversion tradeoff on high-consideration purchases, and worth keeping separate from it. That tradeoff is about which creative wins the click for an expensive item. This is about how long any winning creative, AI-generated or not, keeps winning before the same audience gets tired of seeing it.

19.3 versus 7.1 days is the practical version of the number that matters to a media buyer: how often the creative rotation has to happen before performance craters, which is a direct input to how much creative production a campaign actually needs to sustain, not just how good the best single piece of creative is.

If a top-performing ad's CTR has been declining over the last 1 to 2 weeks with no targeting change, the more likely explanation is decay, not audience mismatch, and the fix is a new variant in rotation, not a new targeting strategy.

Takeaway: creative fatigue decays performance on a timeline, 7.1 days static versus 19.3 days with AI-driven rotation in 1 documented analysis. Check the calendar before you blame the targeting.
