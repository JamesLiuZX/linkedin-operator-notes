## Draft

Ask legal at the end and you do not get a delay.

You get a redesign. In a regulated venue those are 2 different line items with 2 different owners, and only 1 of them fits in a launch plan.

{{REBUILD_SENTENCE: state the rough multiple. Example shape: "The version that came back took about 3 times the original build." One sentence, no jurisdiction specifics.}}

The reason is structural rather than bureaucratic. What can be listed, how it resolves, who can participate, and what can be marketed are not footnotes on a finished product. They are the shape of the product. Change any one of them after the UX is built and the UX is wrong, not late.

Users never experience "pending regulatory review". They experience a venue that feels arbitrary. Available here, restricted there, unexplained everywhere, which reads as a product that might move the floor under them again.

{{COST_SENTENCE: what you got wrong about sequencing and what it cost. One sentence.}}

The job in the middle is translation, and it is a product job. Legal speaks in risk. Users speak in outcomes. Engineering speaks in state machines. Nobody is being difficult. There is no shared vocabulary, and building one is faster than rebuilding the feature.

The practical version: take the next category to your compliance partner as a prototype rather than a deck, early enough that the answer can still change the design instead of only the launch date.

Takeaway: treat compliance partners like design partners, with prototypes, before the UX is built around an assumption.
