# Getting past the cringe barrier

This is the real blocker — not "what to post."

## What's actually going on

Looking down on LinkedIn posters is often **status protection**:

> If LinkedIn is cringe, I never risk being judged for trying.

Your sharp friends may reinforce that. They're optimizing for in-group taste. You're optimizing for **out-group opportunity** (roles, intros, credibility with strangers who hire and fund).

Those are different games. Stop playing friends' game on LinkedIn.

## Reframes that work

1. **Operator notes ≠ thought leadership**  
   You're writing the same thing as a good eng blog post. Different platform, same honesty bar.

2. **Write for strangers**  
   The audience that matters has never been in your group chat.

3. **Cringe is usually a writing problem**  
   Vague hustle posts are embarrassing. Specific tradeoff posts rarely are — even to you. If you feel cringe, cut the performance, keep the mechanism.

4. **Embarrassment budget**  
   Pre-commit: posts 1–8 will feel awkward. Success = published. Likes are irrelevant for 30 days.

5. **Identity split is allowed**  
   LinkedIn voice = calm technical PM. Friends = whatever. Different rooms, different norms.

## Tactics (use these)

- [ ] Draft offline for a few days; only publish the least performative versions
- [ ] Ban list taped mentally: humbled, hot take, agree?, emoji walls, fake vulnerability
- [ ] Tell one trusted peer: "I'm writing operator notes for 90 days — roast the writing, not the fact I'm posting"
- [ ] Mute notifications 48 hours after posting if you spiral
- [ ] If friends tease: "I'm optimizing for opportunities outside our chat." Then change subject. Don't debate.

## The 5-second publish rule

When the draft passes positioning + compliance tests:

1. Open LinkedIn  
2. Paste  
3. Post  
4. Close app  
5. Go do something physical for 10 minutes  

No re-reading. No editing the hook 12 times after publish (fix typos later if needed).

## If you relapse into "this is beneath me"

Read your own unfair advantage list. People with less experience post confidently every day. Silence doesn't make you higher status — it just makes you invisible to the market you're trying to enter.
