## Draft

A study of over 500M ad impressions, from researchers at Columbia, Harvard, and Carnegie Mellon, found AI-generated ads beat human-made ones on click-through rate: 0.76% against 0.65%.

The same study found conversion rates for those same AI ads dropped 8% on high-consideration purchases. On purchases over $500, the drop widened to 14%.

Same 500M impressions. Neither number is wrong. They're answers to 2 different questions a single "AI ads win" headline collapses into 1.

A click costs a viewer nothing, half a second of curiosity. A $500 purchase after that click requires trusting the product enough to pay for it, and that's exactly where AI creative's advantage reverses. The novelty and slight uncanniness that earns a click doesn't obviously earn the trust a big purchase runs on.

On lower-priced products, the same study found full conversion parity. The gap isn't a general AI-creative weakness. It's specific to expensive, high-consideration decisions, and it's invisible if you're only looking at CTR.

The reason CTR is the number that gets reported: it's available same-day from the ad platform's own dashboard. A trustworthy conversion number for a $500 purchase, with a sales cycle that can run weeks, takes longer to settle. The easy number wins the reporting race regardless of which number actually decided if the campaign made money.

Takeaway: a click and a sale are different customer decisions. Check the conversion rate before calling a CTR win a win, especially above $500.
