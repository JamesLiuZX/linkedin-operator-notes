# The duty stays with you

Covering 24 to 25 Nov 2026, plus one launch from late on 23 Nov, US time, that the last edition missed. Four changes, and each one hands a duty back to the team that ships: a regulator that won't accept "we're just the technology", a vendor that moves the misuse review into your account, a protocol that tells hosts to vet what servers send, and a coalition of attorneys general arguing that a privacy promise survives a sale. Plus two courses and my read. Every number links to where it came from.

## 1. The FCA says a "technology provider" label won't exempt an AI agent from authorisation

**What happened.** On Tuesday 24 Nov the UK's Financial Conduct Authority published "Our approach to Agentic AI", a page it says it will update regularly. It is engaging with firms that offer personal AI agent products to UK consumers, and it says those products can blur the line between regulated and unregulated activity. As AI moves from giving information to "initiating transactions, moving money" and "arranging borrowing or making investment decisions", firms must understand which of those activities need authorisation. For technology providers, the page notes that the boundary of regulation "is set by Parliament", and that describing your business as a technology provider does not remove the need for authorisation to provide financial services in the UK. It names what to check, for example whether you are offering "payments services", "issuing financial promotions", "providing an authorised information service", "engaging in credit broking or intermediation" or "facilitating or offering financial advice". The FCA has powers to intervene when regulated services are offered without authorisation, and it "will not hesitate to use them if necessary".

**Why it matters.** The test is what your agent does, not what your company calls itself. An agent that initiates a payment, introduces a loan or recommends an investment can be carrying on one of those five activities, and the page adds that a product can need regulation at launch or grow into it later. Firms on the receiving end get questions too: whether it is appropriate to "accept payments instructed by an AI agent" or from a digital wallet an agent controls, how they will know they are dealing with an agent, what it is allowed to do, and how customers get help when it goes wrong. Senior managers stay responsible for AI in their area whether it was built in-house or bought, and the Consumer Duty applies. AI providers are expected to give "clear, proportionate and consistent disclaimers" backed by "effective operational safeguards". If you ship an agent to UK consumers, map every action it can take against those five activities before launch, and use the FCA's pre-application support if any of them fits.

**The catch.** This is existing regulation applied to agents, not a new rulebook: the page calls the framework "principles-based and outcomes-focused" and lists no consultation, deadline or rule change. The five activities are examples, not a complete list, and the page says nothing specific about kill switches, record-keeping or agents dealing with other agents. It says it intends to run "a dedicated Agentic Policy Sprint", with no date. It covers the UK only, and it points to a joint statement on personal AI agents from the UK's digital regulators, the DRCF, which I have not read.

Source: [FCA, "Our approach to Agentic AI", first published 24 Nov 2026](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach/agentic-ai)

## 2. Claude's most capable models can keep their logs in your cloud, and your team answers the flags

**What happened.** On 23 Nov Anthropic opened Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS) in public beta on the Claude Platform, Claude Platform on AWS, Amazon Bedrock and Claude on Google Cloud, with Microsoft Foundry "coming soon". AWS's post the same day made its trusted access program API generally available on Bedrock, with EFS and a second option, Enterprise Data Controls (EDC), in public preview, for "Claude Fable, Claude Mythos, and Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 used through the Cyber Verification Program". Under EFS on Bedrock, prompts and outputs go to an S3 bucket in your own account, encrypted with your own KMS key, and AWS says Bedrock "keeps no copy". Only automated safety monitoring reads them, and AWS says no AWS or Anthropic person accesses them. When the monitoring finds a risk, Anthropic says it "creates a Safety Case for you to review". The background: since 9 Jun 2026, prompts and outputs sent to Anthropic's Mythos-class models have been retained for 30 days, per its support page, and Anthropic has said that enterprises "found it difficult to use models with data retention".

**Why it matters.** The retention moves into your account, and the misuse review comes with it. Anthropic's docs say your team must respond to a Safety Case "within 7 calendar days", marking it `violation` or `no_violation`, and that "if a Safety Case stays unanswered, Anthropic may suspend access". The bucket becomes a production dependency: AWS says Bedrock stops serving covered models 24 hours after it loses access to your key or bucket, and Anthropic's docs describe a grace period of up to 24 hours before requests can be refused. AWS's comparison table lists an interim zero data retention exception for current Fable models, with no safety monitoring, "through at least June 30, 2027", and says joining the preview turns that exception off for the accounts you add. Before you enrol, name the person who answers Safety Cases, alarm on changes to the key and bucket policies, and decide who inside your company may read that bucket, because it will hold flagged misuse cases about your own users.

**The catch.** Both are a beta and a preview, and Anthropic says settings can change. On Bedrock, EDC cases "aren't delivered during the public preview", storage is configured one Region at a time, and enrolment goes through your AWS account team. On Anthropic's platform, EFS does not cover code execution containers, Files API uploads, Batch API results or Claude Managed Agents sessions. Anthropic doesn't charge for EFS and AWS names no separate fee, but your cloud bills the storage, requests and key operations. I read the announcements and docs, not the contract terms that govern the beta.

Sources: [Anthropic, "Zero data retention with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards is now in beta"](https://claude.com/resources/articles/zero-data-retention-with-enterprise-frontier-safeguards) · [Anthropic, EFS announcement, 1 Sep](https://www.anthropic.com/news/enterprise-frontier-safeguards) · [AWS, "Introducing the Amazon Bedrock Trusted Access API"](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/introducing-the-amazon-bedrock-trusted-access-api-with-anthropics-enterprise-frontier-safeguards-and-enterprise-data-controls/) · [EFS docs: review Safety Cases](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/enterprise-frontier-safeguards/flagged-cases) · [Anthropic, data retention for covered models](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15425996-data-retention-practices-for-covered-models)

## 3. MCP servers can now ship skills to Claude, and the spec says to treat them as untrusted

**What happened.** On 24 Nov, as the Model Context Protocol turned two, Anthropic put Skills over MCP in public beta in Claude and Claude Code. An MCP server can now ship skills alongside its tools, and they update with the server, so users have nothing separate to install. It is on by default for Pro, Max and Ultra plans, available to Team plans once an admin enables it, and "coming soon" to Enterprise. Tasks, which let a server answer a tool call with a handle the client polls, and Elicitation, which lets a server ask the user for input through a form or a link, are now generally available for Pro, Max and Ultra and rolling out to Team and Enterprise. Anthropic names Supabase, Railway and GitHub as servers that use Elicitation to confirm costs or ask before destructive changes. It says MCP "now sees over 700 million monthly SDK downloads", and the MCP maintainers say the SDKs have crossed 5 billion downloads in total.

**Why it matters.** A skill is a folder with a `SKILL.md` file, and under the extension's spec it "MAY contain" scripts as well as references and examples. The extension's overview tells hosts to "treat skill content as untrusted input", and says a changed, added or removed file revokes approval, so the host must ask again before loading or running it. In practice, the vendor behind a server you connected can now change the instructions Claude follows when it uses that vendor's tools. On a Team plan, decide which servers may ship skills before an admin switches the feature on, and read what each one ships. If you build a server that can move money or delete data, put an Elicitation step in front of that action so a person confirms it. If you implemented the experimental tasks from the 2025-11-25 spec, plan the migration, because the final Tasks extension "removes the version of tasks specified in the 2025-11-25 release from the core protocol."

**The catch.** Skills over MCP is a beta, and the MCP overview says "SDK and host support is still being implemented". Anthropic's announcement says nothing about what a server-shipped skill may do inside Claude or how Claude shows a changed one, and the spec notes that its file digests "are unsigned and supplied by the same server that supplies the content". The download figures are counts by Anthropic and the MCP project, and a download is not a deployment. File Uploads is "rolling out soon" in Claude, while the extension behind it is still a working-group draft.

Sources: [Anthropic, "MCP servers can now ship skills, run tasks, and more with Claude"](https://claude.com/resources/articles/skills-over-mcp-tasks-and-more) · [MCP blog, "Two Years of MCP Builders"](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/mcp-turns-two/) · [Skills extension overview](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/extensions/skills/overview) · [SEP-2640, Skills Extension](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/seps/2640-skills-extension) · [SEP-2663, Tasks Extension](https://tasks.extensions.modelcontextprotocol.io/seps/2663-tasks-extension)

## 4. 42 attorneys general want to stop the auction of Character.AI's private chats

**What happened.** On 24 Nov California's Attorney General, Rob Bonta, said a bipartisan coalition of 42 attorneys general is asking the bankruptcy court to reject a proposed sale of Character.AI's platform. "All chats, together with users' personal data, including IP addresses and other metadata, are set to be sold at auction on December 1, 2026," the release says. They come from more than 21 million US accounts, and more than 2.1 million of those are registered to minors. The sale also covers the risk scores Character.AI gave users it flagged. The only bidder disclosed so far, Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC, was set up as a Delaware company on 13 Nov 2026, one day before Character.AI picked it as the base bid, and its contract names no parent company or investors. Character.AI's privacy policy told users "we do not sell personal information for monetary consideration", yet under the proposed terms the buyer "would not have to follow Character.AI's privacy policies or honor users' requests to opt out or delete their data."

**Why it matters.** Every chat log you keep is an asset in a sale and a liability in court. The states ask the court to deny the proposed terms, reject Smirks as the lead bidder and refuse its $350,000 break-up fee, and at a minimum to leave kids' chats and other sensitive data out of any sale. If your product stores AI conversations, and finance products hold some of the most sensitive ones, read what your privacy policy says happens to that data in a merger, a sale or an insolvency, and check that your retention schedule actually deletes chats you no longer need. The same goes for any risk score or misuse flag you compute about a user: it travels with the data.

**The catch.** Nothing is decided. This is the states' filing, the court has not ruled, and the auction is still set for 1 Dec. The release names no court or case number, and its own count wobbles: it says 42 attorneys general, while Bonta's quote in it says 45. TechCrunch, which reported the coalition on 25 Nov, says it is unclear what the buyer plans to do with the chats. I read the California release, not the filing itself.

Sources: [California Attorney General, 24 Nov](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-opposes-asset-sale-characterai-amid-child-safety-concerns) · [TechCrunch, 25 Nov](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/state-ags-fight-character-ais-plan-to-auction-off-users-private-chats/)

## My read

Each item this week moves a duty back onto the people who ship. The FCA won't let "we're just the technology" carry the authorisation question. Anthropic and AWS moved the logs for their most capable models into your account, and the misuse review moved with them, on a seven-day clock. MCP now lets a server's vendor change the instructions your model follows, and its own spec puts the vetting on the host. And 42 attorneys general are arguing that a privacy promise binds a company even while it is being sold off. None of these duties can be handed to a vendor, a protocol or a buyer. Each one also turns into something concrete you can check this week: a map of what your agent does, a named owner for Safety Cases, a list of servers allowed to ship skills, and the change-of-control clause in your privacy policy. Two things I could not check: how Claude shows a changed skill to the person who approved it, and what the Character.AI filing asks for beyond the California release.

Takeaway: give every duty your AI product carries a named owner this week: the activity map, the Safety Case queue, the skills list and the privacy promise.
