## Draft

Users will forgive a bad beat.

They will not forgive feeling played.

I’ve watched people lose money and come back the next day. I’ve also watched people win once, feel something off about the venue, and never open the app again.

Same balance change. Opposite story in their head.

A prediction market product has three trust surfaces. Most teams obsess over one and leak the other two.

**1) Price has to feel real**  
If spreads are garbage or you can’t exit without eating glass, users learn the product is a toy. Fancy UI won’t save it. Fewer liquid markets beat forty decorative ones.

**2) Resolution is UX**  
People accept losing. They cannot accept not understanding how the outcome was decided. Ambiguous rules are not a legal detail. They are a UX defect with financial consequences. If a non-lawyer can’t explain the settle in 30 seconds, the market isn’t ready.

**3) The app has to stop surprising people**  
Fees after confirm. PnL that doesn’t match the math in your head. Orders that hang in the only five minutes that mattered. In a social app, surprise is annoying. In a market, surprise feels extractive.

You can grow with campaigns.  
You cannot grow past broken trust surfaces.

When a roadmap item shows up, I ask one thing:

Does this make prices feel more real, settles feel cleaner, or the app feel less surprising?

If no, it’s probably decoration for a spike.

Build for the user who lost money fairly and still came back.  
That person is the whole game. Everyone else is traffic.
