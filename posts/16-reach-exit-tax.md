## Draft

A link sitting in the body of a LinkedIn post costs roughly 60% of that post's reach, compared to the identical post with no link in it.

Not the topic. Not the writing. The address, four inches down the page, in the first comment instead of the body.

The mechanism is not mysterious. The platform's ranking rewards time spent on it, and a link is an invitation to leave. It cannot tell a good destination from a bad one, so it discounts any post that visibly asks a reader to click away before it has finished deciding how far to show that post to anyone else.

Two more numbers worth knowing, from the same research pass, DataSlayer's and SocialBee's. A post that holds someone past 61 seconds correlates with 15.6% engagement, versus 1.2% for one that loses them in the first 3, about 13 times over. A substantive comment counts roughly 2x a like in the ranking signal, and a generic one-word comment gets discounted as inauthentic.

None of this is a reason to write worse, and it is worth saying as a rule rather than a suggestion. A hook engineered purely for the first three seconds, with nothing behind it, shows the same pattern on the way down as on the way up. The platform measures attention. It does not measure whether the post kept its promise.

Takeaway: move today's outbound link from the body to the first comment and change nothing else. It is worth more reach than most rewrites, for one formatting decision.
