## Draft

The AI avatar tool with the smoothest lip-sync and the widest language support needed a re-render on roughly 70% of its outputs before they were ad-ready, in 1 hands-on comparison. The rougher, less impressive-looking competitor needed it on 35%.

That's not a coincidence. It's the whole mechanism of why UGC advertising works.

UGC's entire premise is that a stranger, not a studio, is talking to you. Slightly off framing, a phone camera instead of a rig, a voice pitched at 1 friend instead of an audience, none of that is an accident. It's the signal that tells a viewer's attention this isn't a traditional ad, which is exactly why it earns attention a polished ad doesn't.

An avatar tool built to look as broadcast-smooth as possible is optimizing directly against that signal. The smoother it looks, the more it reads as exactly what the format was designed to not look like, and audiences on TikTok and Instagram have seen enough AI video by now to recognize the tell fast.

Arcads, an AI UGC-ad startup that raised a $16M seed round in December 2025 and now serves over 6,000 clients a month, built its 300-actor library around direct-response performance, not polish. That's why it had the lowest rework rate of the 3 tools compared, not despite having the smallest, least flashy feature set.

Takeaway: UGC's whole job is to not look produced. An avatar tool optimized for polish is optimizing against the one property that made the format work.
