# The clocks got shorter

Covering 26 to 27 Nov 2026, a US holiday window with little new from vendors. So three of these four items were published on 24 and 25 Nov and reported over the holiday, and the last edition missed them; the fourth is a deadline that lands on Monday. Each one is a clock: how long an attacker's agent needs, how long you have to patch, how long your agent has to change doors, and how long until an API you may use stops. Plus two courses and my read. Every number links to where it came from.

## 1. Switzerland confirmed its first AI-agent attacks, and every one it knows of got in

**What happened.** On 24 Nov the Swiss National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) said that "in recent days" it had received its first reports of attacks on organisations in Switzerland carried out with the help of an AI agent. "Various organisations and operators of critical infrastructure across at least two sectors have been affected", and "In all cyberattacks known to the NCSC, the use of AI agents led to the successful compromise of the victim's IT systems." The attackers used what the NCSC calls agentic offensive frameworks: agents that "independently search for vulnerabilities, select suitable attack tools, evaluate the results, and plan and execute the next steps". They got in through standard or easily guessed passwords and known vulnerabilities in systems connected to the internet. The NCSC says the number of such agents publicly accessible on the internet, naming ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI, "has almost doubled since October 2026", and it expects more rapid, successful attacks.

**Why it matters.** None of this needed a new kind of bug. The agent does the old work, finding the exposed system and the guessable password, in a fraction of the time a person needs, so the defence is the old checklist on a much shorter clock. The NCSC asks that critical security updates for systems accessible via the internet be installed "as soon as possible", and "no later than 24 hours after release". A team that patches on a monthly cycle leaves weeks open for an agent that needs minutes. The NCSC also asks for MFA or passkeys on every internal resource reachable from the internet, "such as webmail, VPN", least privilege, geo-blocking or allow lists in front of exposed systems, detection of unusual behaviour in identities and workloads, and regular penetration tests. Before Monday, find out how long your last critical patch on an internet-facing system took from release to install.

**The catch.** The page gives no count of attacks, names no sector and no victim, and says nothing about which models the attackers ran. It is an advisory for Switzerland, not a rule anywhere. Reuters reported the statement on 26 Nov; I read the NCSC's own page, not the Reuters story.

Source: [NCSC, "Cyberattacks involving AI agents: First confirmed cases in Switzerland", 24 Nov 2026](https://www.bacs.admin.ch/en/26-ki-agenten-1-en)

## 2. Microsoft named six groups using one open-source AI attack agent

**What happened.** On 25 Nov Microsoft Threat Intelligence published "What defenders need to know about ARTEX, the agentic AI framework threat actors abuse". ARTEX is "an AI-driven penetration testing platform": a Go backend that coordinates several LLM agents, with a planner that issues intents and workers that carry them out "with a real shell and offensive tools". It is "primarily a web application penetration testing tool", built to find SQL injection, server-side template injection, XXE, file upload flaws, authentication bypass and IDOR. A GitHub user published it as open source in July 2026; the original developer removed the code in October, forks carried on, and in November the China-based security company ThreatBook claimed ownership and began selling access to "ARTEX Pro". Microsoft says that as of November 2026 both state-sponsored and financially motivated groups, Storm-0940, Storm-3358, Onyx Sleet, Pearl Sleet, Crystal Typhoon and Salt Typhoon, have added it to their tooling, and that it "can advance from reconnaissance to persistent, privileged access to a target organization in a matter of minutes". In one Storm-0940 campaign, Microsoft "recorded more than 151,000 failed sign-ins, peaking at 2,900 failures per minute". An earlier Microsoft post on the same tool, on 6 Nov, was titled "ARTEX abuse expands as cybercriminals target financial sector".

**Why it matters.** The attacker's agent works at machine speed, and that is also how you see it. Microsoft calls "the bursts of machine speed activity" the most durable signal, and says that by default ARTEX "does not take any measures to obfuscate its malicious activity": its requests carry user agents such as `artex-enrich/` and `norma/`, although attackers can change those. The operator can plug in "virtually any large language model", including open-weight models with their refusals stripped out, so no model vendor's usage policy stands in the way. For a product that moves money, the changes are concrete: put your web application firewall in prevention mode, add rate limiting to cap how many requests one client can send in a short time, apply Conditional Access to "every user and workload identity", and "keep application secrets out of code and replace any that leak". For APIs, Microsoft points to the OWASP API Top 10. It also reports that "honeypots, decoys, tarpits, and fake wins" pulled attack agents away from real targets and, in its testing, "helped reduce the attack traffic reaching real assets".

**The catch.** This is Microsoft's own threat intelligence, and its mitigation list leans on Microsoft products such as Defender, Sentinel and Entra. The part I read gives no count of victims, and the deception result comes from Microsoft's own testing. The post is a 30-minute read: I read a little under half of it word for word, up to the advice on application secrets, and the rest only in summary, so nothing here comes from the later sections. I did not read the 6 Nov post, only its title.

Sources: [Microsoft Threat Intelligence, 25 Nov 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/25/what-defenders-need-to-know-artex-agentic-ai-framework-threat-actors-abuse/) · [Microsoft, "ARTEX abuse expands as cybercriminals target financial sector", 6 Nov 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/06/artex-abuse-expands-as-cybercriminals-target-financial-sector/)

## 3. Google is giving AI agents their own door into Gmail and Drive, with a deadline

**What happened.** On 24 Nov, according to the Cloud Security Alliance's daily briefing and to write-ups by the developer Flavio Copes and others, Google made its hosted Google Workspace MCP servers and its Workspace command-line tool generally available and added a Workspace AI Tools Addendum to the Google APIs Terms of Service. The addendum, as Copes quotes it, covers any tool that "is or is driven by an AI Agent, an AI Harness or an AI model or service employing an AI model". The CSA's briefing says that from 1 Feb 2027 such agents can reach Workspace only through the Workspace MCP servers or the CLI, not the REST APIs, and the write-ups say software with no AI in the loop keeps using the REST APIs as before. Google's own setup guide, which I read, covers the generally available servers (Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Calendar, Chat, Tasks and People) and says "User-delegated OAuth 2.0 client IDs that call the Google Workspace MCP servers must have Use this client for an AI-powered agent selected" in Google Auth Platform. If a client also serves non-agent features, the guide says to create a new OAuth client ID for the agent. Three of the write-ups add that billing for agent usage above a free daily allowance starts on 1 Apr 2027.

**Why it matters.** If your product lets an AI agent read a customer's Gmail, Drive or Calendar, the integration has 65 days to change doors. That is an engineering migration and a consent change. A separate OAuth client flagged as an agent means a separate consent screen for your users and a separate line for their admins to approve or block, which is the point: Workspace admins get to tell your agent apart from your app. Start with an inventory of every place an LLM touches Workspace through the REST APIs, move the busiest one first, and put the 1 Apr 2027 billing date in next year's cost plan.

**The catch.** I could not read the addendum itself or Google's agent policy page, which refused automated readers, and I could not open the Help Net Security report the CSA cites. So the 24 Nov date, the 1 Feb 2027 deadline and the 1 Apr 2027 billing date come from the CSA's briefing and from write-ups that agree with each other but are not Google; the OAuth rule and the server list come from Google's setup guide. Copes also reads the addendum as limiting how long you may store data derived from Workspace, such as embeddings and memory indexes, to 30 days. That is his reading and I have not checked it, so read the addendum before you design agent memory around Workspace data.

Sources: [Google, "Configure the Google Workspace MCP servers"](https://developers.google.com/workspace/guides/configure-mcp-servers) · [Cloud Security Alliance, CISO Daily Briefing, 27 Nov 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/ciso-daily-briefing-20261127/) · [Flavio Copes, 26 Nov 2026](https://flaviocopes.com/google-workspace-mcp/) · [Paul Lovelace on DEV, 25 Nov 2026](https://dev.to/paullovelace/google-workspace-mcp-servers-are-generally-available-and-rest-api-access-for-ai-agents-ends-feb-1-1n43)

## 4. OpenAI's prompt objects and Agent Builder stop on Monday, and three more cut-offs follow by 15 Dec

**What happened.** OpenAI's deprecations page lists four cut-off dates between Monday 30 Nov and 15 Dec. On 30 Nov, `/v1/prompts` and reusable prompt objects shut down, with the advice "Move prompt content into your application code"; the Evals dashboard, API and graders become "Read-only", with the advice "Migrate to Promptfoo"; and Agent Builder shuts down, replaced by the "Agents SDK or ChatGPT Workspace Agents", while "ChatKit remains available". On 1 Dec, gpt-image-1-mini, gpt-image-1.5 and chatgpt-image-latest go, replaced by gpt-image-2.6-sunburst or gpt-image-2.6-flare. On 11 Dec, gpt-5, gpt-5-mini, gpt-5-nano, gpt-5-pro, o3 and o3-pro go, replaced by gpt-6.1-sol, gpt-6.1-terra and gpt-6-luna. And on 15 Dec, "no organization can create new self-serve fine-tuning jobs". None of this is new: the notices are dated between 7 May and 11 Jun 2026. It is the landing that is new.

**Why it matters.** Six months of notice is easy to lose, and Monday is when it bites. A prompt stored in OpenAI's dashboard is a dependency your repository cannot see, so it is the one most likely to be forgotten, and OpenAI's own advice is to move it into application code, where it can be reviewed and versioned like everything else. If your team built an agent in Agent Builder, or kept its regression tests in Evals, those need a new home before they stop or freeze. The model dates reach further than they look, because "Fine-tuned versions of the models listed above follow the same shutdown schedule as their underlying base models", so a fine-tune on gpt-5-mini stops with its base on 11 Dec. This weekend, search your code and config for prompt IDs, Agent Builder workflows and the model names on the list.

**The catch.** The page does not say what happens to stored prompts or Agent Builder workflows after Monday, and I found no official export tool, so copy out what you need now. It names replacement models, not their prices, so check what the move costs before 11 Dec. Suppliers count too: ask any vendor whose product runs on gpt-5 or o3 which model it will run on after 11 Dec.

Sources: [OpenAI, Deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations) · [OpenAI, "Migrate from prompt objects"](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompting/migrate-from-prompt-object)

## New courses worth an evening

- **OWASP API Security Top 10: Secure Your APIs** · freeCodeCamp on YouTube · free. Corey Ball, author of Hacking APIs, takes 1.5 hours over all ten risks on the 2023 list, from broken object level authorization (the IDOR bugs ARTEX is built to find) to unrestricted resource consumption and server-side request forgery. Not new: freeCodeCamp posted it on 30 Aug 2023. It is on the list because Microsoft's ARTEX advice for APIs points to this one. [YouTube](https://youtu.be/YYe0FdfdgDU)

One hour from Anthony Aragues, head of APIsec Labs, on CORS, error disclosure, information leaks, secure cookies, path traversal and rate limiting, the control that slows an agent sending thousands of requests a minute. Not new either: posted on 19 Sep 2024. [YouTube](https://youtu.be/2-DmxENygRc)

## My read

Every item this week is a clock, and each one is shorter than the habit it replaces. An attacker's agent needs minutes to get from reconnaissance to privileged access, by Microsoft's account. Switzerland's NCSC wants critical patches on internet-facing systems within 24 hours, which a monthly patch cycle misses by weeks. Google gives agents that read Workspace data until 1 Feb 2027 to change doors, and OpenAI's six months of notice run out on Monday. Underneath is one shift: agents, yours and the attacker's, now get treated as their own kind of traffic. They get their own door into Google, their own fingerprint in Microsoft's logs and their own patch clock in Switzerland. For a regulated product, the useful question is which of your clocks is still set for human speed: patching, credential rotation, access reviews, vendor change notices. Two things I could not check: the text of Google's addendum, and the later sections of Microsoft's post, which I saw only in summary.

Takeaway: this week, set a 24-hour rule for critical patches on internet-facing systems, mark your agents' Google OAuth clients as agents, and move OpenAI prompts into your code before Monday.
